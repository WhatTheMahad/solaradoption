Solar Adoption in Pakistan — 2026

Tracking Pakistan's rooftop solar net-metering boom by DISCO, and the February 2026 shift from net metering to net billing. As of mid-2025, Pakistan had roughly 283,000 active net-metering connections totaling about 6.1 GW of installed rooftop capacity — up from 4.9 GW at end-2024 — yet still under 3% of national grid supply. Pakistan imported about 17 GW of solar panels in 2024.

The February 2026 policy shift: net metering → net billing

NEPRA's Prosumer Regulations 2026 replaced net metering with net billing for new rooftop-solar consumers:

  • Export buyback: Rs 22–27/kWh → ~Rs 11/kWh
  • Billing basis: unit-for-unit offset → imports and exports priced separately
  • Contract length: 7 years → 5 years
  • Typical payback: 3–4 years → 6–8 years

Existing net-metering consumers are generally honored for the remainder of their contracts.

FAQ

Where does this data come from?

The backbone is NEPRA's State of the Industry Report and net-metering statistics, cross-checked against Renewables First (PECI tracker, Pakistan Electricity Review 2025), pv-magazine, and Ember.

What changed in February 2026?

NEPRA's Prosumer Regulations 2026 replaced net metering with net billing: exported solar is now bought back at about Rs 11/kWh instead of the retail tariff, contracts shortened from 7 to 5 years, and payback periods roughly doubled to 6–8 years.

What is net metering vs net billing?

Net metering is a unit-for-unit swap valued near the retail rate. Net billing prices imports and exports separately, buying back exports at a lower regulator-set rate.

Why is K-Electric separate from the DISCOs?

K-Electric is Pakistan's only private vertically-integrated utility (Karachi) and runs its own net-metering pipeline, distinct from the ten WAPDA-descended DISCOs.
